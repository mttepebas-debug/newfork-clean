GOAL: Stop Feature Development. Stabilize and Ship the MVP.

Mission

We are changing the project strategy.

The original vision was to build a nationwide compliance engine that supports every Federal, State and Local labor law in the United States.

That vision is NOT cancelled, but it is postponed.

From this point forward your only objective is to make the application completely functional from end to end for California employers only.

Do not continue adding architecture for future states.

Do not add new abstractions.

Do not design for theoretical future scenarios.

Our priority is:

A small system that completely works is infinitely more valuable than a huge system that never reaches production.

⸻

Your first responsibility

Before writing any code, perform a complete engineering review of the current project.

I believe the current codebase has become over-engineered.

Many features exist only partially.

Many UI screens are confusing.

Many flows cannot even be tested because they require information that is difficult to understand.

I want you to review the entire compliance architecture with one question in mind:

“Is this necessary for a California MVP?”

For every feature you find, classify it into one of these categories.

Category A — Required for MVP

Must exist for California production.

Examples:

* California labor law detection
* Federal labor law baseline
* California jurisdiction detection
* Pay Entity
* Location
* Employee
* Time Entries
* Clock In
* Clock Out
* Break calculations
* Regular Hours
* Overtime
* Daily Overtime
* Weekly Overtime
* Payroll Ready Reporting

⸻

Category B — Keep but Hide

Useful later.

Do not delete.

Simply disable or hide from UI.

Examples may include:

* Fair Work Week
* Typical Hours
* Labor Cost projections
* Advanced Compliance Preview
* Future compliance modules
* Alternative Work Week Program (unless required for California MVP)
* Experimental compliance features
* Any unfinished advanced engine

⸻

Category C — Remove from Current Flow

Anything that blocks testing.

Anything confusing.

Anything unfinished.

Anything that requires advanced configuration.

Anything whose absence does not prevent California payroll calculations.

These should be removed from the onboarding flow and moved into future milestones.
